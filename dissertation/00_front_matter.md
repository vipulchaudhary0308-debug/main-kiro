## PREFACE

The choice of a vocation is one of the most consequential decisions in the life of an individual. It shapes not only one's economic future but also one's personal identity, social relationships and overall quality of life. In India, where millions of young people complete their secondary education every year and then face a bewildering array of career choices, the scientific study of vocational interests assumes immense practical importance.

The present dissertation, entitled **"A Comparative Study of the Vocational Interests of Boys and Girls Studying at Secondary Level"**, is an attempt to contribute, in a modest measure, to the body of empirical knowledge in this important area of educational psychology and guidance. The study compares the vocational interest profiles of boys and girls of Classes IX and X, drawn from two representative schools of Meerut district — one rural government and one urban private.

The investigation employs the well-standardised *Vocational Interest Record (Hindi version)* developed by Dr. S.P. Kulshrestha, which measures interest in ten distinct vocational areas. The principal statistical tools used are the Mean, Standard Deviation, *t*-test and Percentage Analysis — techniques that are both rigorous and appropriate for the level of measurement involved.

The dissertation is organised in five chapters. **Chapter I** provides the theoretical and conceptual framework. **Chapter II** reviews the relevant Indian and foreign literature. **Chapter III** describes the research methodology in detail. **Chapter IV** presents the statistical analysis and its interpretation. **Chapter V** summarises the findings, draws conclusions and offers suggestions for teachers, parents, school administrators and future researchers.

It is sincerely hoped that the findings of this study will be useful to the teachers, guidance counsellors, parents, school administrators and educational planners engaged with secondary school education in Meerut and similar districts of western Uttar Pradesh. The investigator is fully aware that every research effort has its limitations; the present study is no exception. The limitations have been duly acknowledged in the last chapter.

Constructive suggestions from teachers, scholars and readers for the improvement of this work are welcome.
