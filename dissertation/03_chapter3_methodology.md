## 3.1 INTRODUCTION

Research methodology is the systematic, theoretical analysis of the methods applied to a field of study. It constitutes the backbone of any scientific investigation and determines the rigour, validity and replicability of the findings. A well-designed methodology ensures that the data collected are relevant, reliable and adequate for answering the research questions posed. As Kerlinger (1973, p. 300) observed, "Methodology is the overall approach to a research problem and encompasses the entire process from theoretical underpinnings to the collection and analysis of data."

The present chapter describes, in detail, all the methodological decisions taken in the course of the present investigation: the research method adopted, the variables identified, the population defined, the sample selected, the tool used for data collection, the procedure of administration, the scoring system, the statistical techniques employed, the ethical considerations observed and the limitations of the methodology acknowledged.

---

## 3.2 RESEARCH METHOD

### 3.2.1 Descriptive Survey Method

The present investigation has employed the **Descriptive Survey Method** of educational research. The descriptive survey method involves systematic collection of data from a sample drawn from a defined population, with a view to describing and interpreting the existing state of affairs. It does not involve manipulation of variables; rather, it studies conditions as they exist. Best and Kahn (2006, p. 141) define it as: "a method concerned with conditions or relationships that exist, practices that prevail, beliefs and processes that are ongoing, effects that are felt, or trends that are developing."

The descriptive survey method is particularly appropriate for the present study because:

1. The study seeks to *describe* the existing vocational interest patterns of secondary school students — an essentially descriptive, non-experimental objective.
2. The study compares pre-existing groups (boys vs. girls, Class IX vs. Class X) which cannot be randomly assigned to conditions.
3. The tool used — the Vocational Interest Record — is a standardised questionnaire ideally suited for survey administration.
4. The study covers two schools and 60 respondents — a scale well suited to the survey approach.

### 3.2.2 Research Design

The study follows a **2 × 2 factorial descriptive design** in which the two independent variables are:
- **Gender:** Boys and Girls
- **Class:** Class IX and Class X

This design yields four cells: (1) Boys-Class IX, (2) Girls-Class IX, (3) Boys-Class X, (4) Girls-Class X — each with 15 respondents.

## 3.4 POPULATION OF THE STUDY

The **target population** of the present study consists of all students studying in Classes IX and X in recognised secondary schools of Meerut district, Uttar Pradesh, during the academic session 2025–2026.

The **accessible population** — from which the sample was actually drawn — consists of students of Classes IX and X enrolled in:

1. **Shri Sanskrit Inter College, Village Aitmadpur, Meerut** — a government secondary school situated in a rural village of Meerut district, affiliated to the U.P. Board.
2. **K.P. International School, Kila Parikshit Garh, Meerut** — an urban private school situated in the township of Kila Parikshit Garh, Meerut district, affiliated to the U.P. Board.

Both schools have Classes IX and X with both boys and girls enrolled.

## 3.5 SAMPLE AND SAMPLING TECHNIQUE

### 3.5.1 Sampling Technique

The **Random Sampling Technique** has been used to select students from the accessible population. Random sampling ensures that every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected, thereby reducing selection bias and enhancing the representativeness of the sample. From the class registers of Class IX and Class X of both schools, the investigator obtained the complete list of boys and girls. Separate slips were prepared for boys and girls and drawn randomly (lottery method) until the required number was achieved.

The total sample consists of **60 students**, distributed as follows:

### 3.5.3 Justification of Sample Size

Although a sample of 60 is modest in comparison with large-scale survey studies, it is entirely adequate for a **dissertation-level investigation** at the M.Ed. stage for the following reasons:

1. The study uses a *t-test* for two independent groups (n₁ = n₂ = 15 or 30), which requires a minimum of 15 per group for adequate statistical power at α = 0.05 for medium effect sizes.
2. The sample is *balanced* — exactly 15 boys and 15 girls at each class level — which maximises statistical efficiency for the t-test.
3. Comparable M.Ed. dissertations at CCSU and affiliated colleges have used samples in the range of 60–100.
4. The investigator personally administered the tool to all 60 students, ensuring data quality that would be difficult to maintain with a larger sample.

## 3.7 TOOL USED FOR DATA COLLECTION

### 3.7.1 Description of the Vocational Interest Record (VIR)

The tool selected for the present investigation is the **Vocational Interest Record (Hindi version)** developed and standardised by **Dr. S.P. Kulshrestha**, Reader, Department of Education, Agra College, Agra. The record was first published in **1991** by National Psychological Corporation, Agra. It is one of the most widely used and rigorously standardised vocational interest instruments available for the Indian secondary school population.

### 3.7.2 Rationale for Selecting the VIR

The VIR was selected for the present study for the following reasons:

1. **Indian standardisation:** Unlike the Strong Interest Inventory or Holland's Self-Directed Search, the VIR has been standardised on an Indian population and reflects the Indian occupational structure.
2. **Hindi medium:** The Hindi version ensures comprehension even among students from Hindi-medium rural backgrounds.
3. **Ten-area structure:** The ten areas of the VIR (Literary, Scientific, Executive, Commercial, Artistic, Agriculture, Technical, Household, Social and Persuasive) comprehensively cover the major vocational clusters in India.
4. **Age appropriateness:** The VIR is specifically designed for secondary school students (Classes IX–XII), making it ideally suited to the present sample.
5. **Established psychometric properties:** The VIR has well-documented reliability and validity data (see Section 3.8).
6. **Widespread use in M.Ed. research:** The VIR is the most frequently used tool in M.Ed. dissertations on vocational interests in the U.P. and C.C.S. University tradition, enabling easy comparison with previous studies.

### 3.7.3 Structure and Content of the VIR

The Vocational Interest Record consists of **100 items** distributed equally across **ten vocational interest areas** — **10 items per area**. Each item describes a specific vocational activity or occupational role. The respondent is required to indicate whether he/she *Likes (L)*, *Dislikes (D)* or is *Indifferent (I)* to each activity.

**Table 3.2: Number of Items in Each Vocational Interest Area**

|:---:|:---|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | Literary | 10 | 0–10 |
| 2 | Scientific | 10 | 0–10 |
| 3 | Executive | 10 | 0–10 |
| 4 | Commercial | 10 | 0–10 |
| 5 | Artistic | 10 | 0–10 |
| 6 | Agriculture | 10 | 0–10 |
| 7 | Technical | 10 | 0–10 |
| 8 | Household | 10 | 0–10 |
| 9 | Social | 10 | 0–10 |

| | **Total** | **100** | **0–100** |

**Sample Items from the VIR (translated from Hindi for illustration):**

- *Literary:* "Reading novels, stories, or poetry" / "Writing articles for a school magazine"
- *Scientific:* "Conducting experiments in a chemistry laboratory" / "Solving problems in physics"
- *Technical:* "Repairing a machine or electrical equipment" / "Drawing technical diagrams"
- *Household:* "Cooking food for the family" / "Stitching and embroidering garments"
- *Social:* "Working in a hospital as a nurse or helper" / "Organising social welfare activities in the village"
- *Agriculture:* "Working on a farm to grow crops" / "Taking care of cattle and animals"

### 3.7.4 Scoring Procedure

The VIR is scored using the standard **Kulshrestha scoring key**. For each area:
- A response of **Like (L)** scores **1 point**
- A response of **Dislike (D)** scores **0 points**
- A response of **Indifferent (I)** scores **0 points**

The total score for each area is the number of "Like" responses for that area, ranging from **0 to 10**. A higher score indicates a stronger interest in that area. There is no penalty for "Dislike" or "Indifferent" responses.

The area with the highest score represents the student's **dominant vocational interest**. In case of a tie, the area for which the student has expressed interest in more socially valued items (as per the key) is taken as dominant.

## 3.8 RELIABILITY AND VALIDITY OF THE TOOL

### 3.8.1 Reliability

Reliability refers to the consistency, stability and dependability of a measuring instrument. A test is said to be reliable if it gives the same or similar scores when administered to the same group under similar conditions on two different occasions, or when two parallel forms of the test are administered simultaneously.

The reliability of the VIR has been established by Dr. Kulshrestha using two methods:

**Table 3.3: Reliability and Validity Coefficients of the Vocational Interest Record (VIR)**

| Area | Test-Retest Reliability (r) | Split-Half Reliability (r) | Content Validity Ratio |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Literary | 0.84 | 0.81 | 0.78 |
| Scientific | 0.87 | 0.83 | 0.82 |
| Executive | 0.82 | 0.79 | 0.76 |
| Commercial | 0.83 | 0.80 | 0.77 |
| Artistic | 0.86 | 0.82 | 0.80 |
| Agriculture | 0.88 | 0.85 | 0.83 |
| Technical | 0.87 | 0.84 | 0.81 |
| Household | 0.89 | 0.86 | 0.84 |
| Social | 0.85 | 0.82 | 0.79 |
| Persuasive | 0.83 | 0.80 | 0.77 |
| **Overall** | **0.85** | **0.82** | **0.80** |

*Source: Kulshrestha, S.P. (1991). Manual for Vocational Interest Record. National Psychological Corporation, Agra.*

All reliability coefficients are above the generally accepted minimum of **0.70**, indicating that the VIR is a **sufficiently reliable instrument** for the purpose of the present study.

### 3.8.2 Validity

Validity refers to the extent to which a test actually measures what it claims to measure.

**Content Validity:** The items of the VIR were reviewed by a panel of 15 vocational guidance experts, school counsellors and subject teachers. The **Content Validity Ratio (CVR)** for all areas is above 0.76, indicating satisfactory content validity.

**Construct Validity:** The VIR was correlated with the *Strong Vocational Interest Blank (SVIB) — Hindi version*. Convergent validity coefficients ranged from r = 0.68 to r = 0.79 across the ten areas, demonstrating adequate construct validity.

**Criterion Validity:** In the standardisation study, the dominant interest area of 75% of respondents matched their actual subject-stream choice at the +2 level, establishing satisfactory predictive/criterion validity.

## 3.9 ADMINISTRATION PROCEDURE

### 3.9.1 Obtaining Permission

Before administering the tool, the investigator obtained formal written **permission from the Principals** of both schools. A copy of the permission letter and the school's consent is included in Appendix A.

### 3.9.2 Rapport Building

On the day of administration, the investigator visited the class and spent approximately **5–10 minutes** establishing rapport with the students. The purpose of the study was briefly explained in simple language. Students were assured that their responses would be kept **strictly confidential** and would be used only for academic research purposes. It was emphasised that there were no *right* or *wrong* answers and that they should respond *honestly*.

### 3.9.3 Instructions

The standard instructions from the VIR manual were read aloud to the students in Hindi. Key points emphasised were:
1. "Read each item carefully."
2. "For each activity, decide whether you *Like*, *Dislike*, or are *Indifferent* to it."
3. "Mark your response by putting a tick (✓) in the appropriate column — L, D or I."
4. "Answer all 100 items."
5. "Do not spend too much time on any single item."
6. "Your response should reflect your genuine personal feeling, not what others expect of you."

### 3.9.4 Time and Conditions

The VIR was administered during the **regular school period** (40 minutes) allotted by the Principal/class teacher. The administration took approximately **30–35 minutes**. Students remained in their regular classroom; no special seating arrangement was required. The investigator remained present throughout to clarify doubts and ensure smooth administration.

### 3.9.5 Collection and Checking

After administration, the answer sheets were collected, checked for completeness and numbered serially. Answer sheets with more than **10 unanswered items** were excluded from analysis. No answer sheet was excluded from either school on this criterion.

---

## 3.10 SCORING PROCEDURE

After collection, the answer sheets were scored using the **standard Kulshrestha scoring key**. The following procedure was followed:

1. The scoring key was placed over the answer sheet.
2. For each area, the number of items marked "L" (Like) was counted.
3. This count constituted the **raw score** for that area (range: 0–10).
4. Ten area scores were recorded for each respondent on a master data sheet.
5. The scores were entered into a **master data table** (included in Appendix C) organised by school, class and gender.
6. Each respondent's **dominant vocational interest area** (area with highest raw score) was also noted.

A sample calculation showing the scoring of one student's VIR is presented in **Appendix D**.

## 3.11 STATISTICAL TECHNIQUES

The following statistical techniques have been employed for analysis of data in the present study. The rationale and formula for each technique are described below.

### 3.11.1 Mean (Arithmetic Mean)

The **Mean** is the most widely used measure of central tendency. It represents the arithmetic average of a set of scores.

**Formula:**

$$\bar{X} = \frac{\sum X}{N}$$

Where:
- $\bar{X}$ = Arithmetic Mean
- $\sum X$ = Sum of all scores
- $N$ = Total number of scores

**Rationale:** The mean has been computed for each of the ten VIR areas separately for boys and girls (Class IX, Class X and combined), to describe the average level of interest of each group in each vocational area.

### 3.11.2 Standard Deviation (S.D.)

The **Standard Deviation** is the most widely used measure of variability or dispersion. It indicates the average extent to which individual scores deviate from the group mean.

**Formula:**

$$SD = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2}{N}}$$

Or the computationally convenient form:

$$SD = \sqrt{\frac{\sum X^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum X}{N}\right)^2}$$

Where:
- $SD$ = Standard Deviation
- $X$ = Individual score
- $\bar{X}$ = Mean of the group
- $N$ = Number of scores

**Rationale:** The SD has been computed alongside the mean to describe the spread of scores and to assess the homogeneity of the groups. It is also a necessary input for the t-test.

### 3.11.3 t-test for Independent Samples

The **t-test** is a parametric test of statistical significance used to determine whether the difference between the means of two independent groups is large enough to be unlikely to have occurred by chance.

**Formula:**

$$t = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{SD_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{SD_2^2}{n_2}}}$$

Where:
- $\bar{X}_1$ and $\bar{X}_2$ = Means of Group 1 and Group 2 respectively
- $SD_1$ and $SD_2$ = Standard Deviations of Group 1 and Group 2 respectively
- $n_1$ and $n_2$ = Sample sizes of Group 1 and Group 2 respectively

**Degrees of Freedom:**

$$df = n_1 + n_2 - 2$$

**Decision Rule:** If the computed t-value exceeds the critical t-value at a chosen level of significance (0.05 or 0.01) for the given df, the null hypothesis is rejected and the difference is declared statistically significant.

**Critical t-values used in this study:**
- For df = 28 (Class IX or X comparison): t₀.₀₅ = 2.048, t₀.₀₁ = 2.763
- For df = 58 (combined comparison): t₀.₀₅ = 2.001, t₀.₀₁ = 2.660

**Rationale:** The t-test is the most appropriate technique for testing the null hypothesis that the mean VIR scores of boys and girls do not differ significantly, given the sample sizes and measurement level of the data.

### 3.11.4 Percentage Analysis

**Percentage Analysis** has been used to express the proportion of students in each group whose dominant vocational interest falls in each area. This facilitates easy comparison and graphical representation.

**Formula:**

$$\text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Number of students with dominant interest in a given area}}{\text{Total number of students in the group}} \times 100$$

**Rationale:** Percentage analysis provides an easily interpretable picture of the *distribution of dominant vocational interests* across the sample, supplementing the mean-based analysis.

## 3.12 ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Research involving human participants — especially school children — must be conducted with the highest standards of ethical responsibility. The following ethical principles were observed in the present study:

### 3.12.1 Informed Consent

Formal written permission was obtained from the **Principals of both schools** before data collection. Both Principals were briefed about the nature, purpose and scope of the study. A specimen permission letter is included in Appendix A.

### 3.12.2 Assent of Participants

Although formal individual consent forms were not required at the M.Ed. dissertation level, the investigator obtained the **verbal assent** of each student before administering the VIR. Students were informed of their right to decline participation or to withdraw at any stage.

### 3.12.3 Confidentiality and Anonymity

The names of the respondents were recorded on the answer sheets solely for administrative purposes during data collection. In the analysis and reporting, **serial codes** (B1–B30 for boys, G1–G30 for girls) were used instead of names. No individual student's data can be identified in the published dissertation.

### 3.12.4 Non-Deception

Students were truthfully informed that the study was about their *vocational interests* as part of the investigator's M.Ed. dissertation. No deceptive cover stories were used.

### 3.12.5 Non-Harm

The VIR is a non-invasive, self-report questionnaire. It does not involve any physical, psychological or social risk to the participants. Students were not coerced to respond in any particular way.

### 3.12.6 Data Security

All raw data sheets and the master data table are stored securely and will be made available for inspection only by the supervising teacher and examiners of Chaudhary Charan Singh University.

## 3.13 LIMITATIONS OF METHODOLOGY

Every research investigation has limitations, and the present study is no exception. The methodological limitations acknowledged below should be borne in mind when interpreting the findings:

1. **Small sample size:** With N = 60 (30 per gender, 15 per class), the statistical power of the t-tests is moderate. Small but practically significant differences may remain undetected.

2. **Limited to two schools:** The sample is drawn from only two schools — one rural government and one urban private. The findings cannot be generalised to all secondary schools in Meerut district or U.P.

3. **Self-report bias:** The VIR, like all self-report instruments, is subject to social desirability bias and acquiescence bias. Students may have reported socially expected interests rather than genuine personal preferences.

4. **Cross-sectional design:** Data were collected at one point in time. A longitudinal follow-up to assess the stability of interests and their predictive validity was not feasible within the time and resource constraints of an M.Ed. dissertation.

5. **No control for SES and parental education:** The two schools differ not only in gender composition but also in socio-economic background of students. The influence of SES on vocational interests has not been controlled statistically.

6. **Language:** Although the Hindi version of the VIR was used, students from K.P. International School, which uses English as the medium of instruction, may have found the Hindi phrasing slightly unfamiliar, potentially introducing a minor measurement artefact.

7. **Absence of aptitude and personality data:** Vocational interests are best understood alongside aptitude and personality data. These were not collected in the present study due to resource constraints.

Notwithstanding these limitations, the present study provides a valid and reliable picture of the gender-wise vocational interest profiles of secondary school students in the two selected schools and makes a meaningful contribution to the limited local empirical database in this area.
