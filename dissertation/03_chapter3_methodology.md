## 3.1 INTRODUCTION

The selection of an *appropriate methodology* is the very heart of an educational research project. The reliability and validity of the conclusions drawn depend, in the last analysis, on the suitability of the *method*, the representativeness of the *sample*, the soundness of the *tool*, the honesty of the *procedure of data collection* and the appropriateness of the *statistical techniques* used. ## 3.2 METHOD OF THE STUDY

The investigator was interested in studying the vocational interest pattern of the secondary school pupils as it *currently exists* in two contrasting types of schools — one Rural Government school and one Urban Private CBSE school — of Meerut district. There was no intention to manipulate any variable or to observe the effect of any intervention. The study, therefore, is a **non-experimental, descriptive study** that has employed the **survey method** of educational research.

According to **John W. Best** (1989):

> "Descriptive research describes and interprets what is. It is concerned with conditions or relationships that exist, opinions that are held, processes that are going on, effects that are evident, or trends that are developing."

The *survey method* of educational research has been chosen because it allows the investigator to collect data from a *fairly large* and *balanced* sample at one point of time and to draw inferences about the larger population.

The design of the study is best described as a **descriptive comparative design** in which the *vocational interest scores* (the dependent variable) on the ten areas of the V.I.R. have been studied across three independent variables, viz.

1. **Type of School** with two levels — *Rural Government* and *Urban Private CBSE*.
2. **Gender** with two levels — *Boys* and *Girls*.
3. **Class** with two levels — *Class IX* and *Class X*.

The schematic representation of the design is given in Section 3.4 (Table 3.1).

## 3.3 POPULATION OF THE STUDY

The *population* of an investigation is the entire group of individuals to which the findings of the study are to be generalised. In the present study, the population has been defined as:

> *All the students of Class IX and Class X studying in Rural Government and Urban Private CBSE-affiliated secondary schools located in the Meerut district during the academic session 2025–2026.*

According to the *District Information System for Education (U-DISE+)* data of 2023–24, Meerut district has approximately 1,150 secondary schools. Of these, about 65 per cent are located in rural areas and 35 per cent in urban areas. The total enrolment in Classes IX and X in the district is approximately 1,55,000 students. This very large body of students has formed the universe of the present study.

## 3.4 SAMPLE AND SAMPLING TECHNIQUE

It was clearly impossible for one investigator to study the entire population of 1.55 lakh students. The investigator, therefore, has drawn a *representative sample* of **60 students** by means of the **stratified random sampling** technique, in three stages.

**Stage I — Selection of Schools.** First, the investigator selected one school of each of the two extreme types — *Rural Government* and *Urban Private CBSE*. Both schools selected are located within Meerut district. The two schools are:

1. **Rural Government** — Shri Sanskrit Inter College, Aitmadpur, Meerut.
2. **Urban Private CBSE** — K.P. International School, Kila Parikshit Garh, Meerut.

The two schools were selected after consulting the Block Education Officers of the area and obtaining the consent of the respective Principals. Both Principals readily agreed to cooperate.

**Stage II — Selection of Class.** From each of the two schools, the investigator selected the entire *Class IX* and *Class X* sections of the school as the *sampling frame*.

**Stage III — Selection of Students.** From each class of each school, the investigator selected 15 students through the *simple lottery method* from the alphabetical class register, with a *fixed gender ratio* of:

- Class IX — 7 boys + 8 girls = 15 students.
- Class X — 8 boys + 7 girls = 15 students.

The same procedure was followed for both schools. The final sample of **60 students** is thus distributed as follows:

| 2. | Shri Sanskrit Inter College, Aitmadpur (Rural Govt.) | X | 8 | 7 | 15 |

The sample is, accordingly, *perfectly balanced* on the gender dimension (30 boys + 30 girls), on the school dimension (30 Rural Govt. + 30 Urban Pvt.) and on the class dimension (30 Class IX + 30 Class X). Such a balanced sample makes the *t-test* analyses of the next chapter both *easy to compute* and *easy to interpret*.

## 3.5 DESCRIPTION OF THE SAMPLE SCHOOLS

A brief description of the two schools selected in the sample is given below.

### 3.5.1 Shri Sanskrit Inter College, Aitmadpur, Meerut (Rural Government)

This is a U.P. Board recognised, government-aided inter college located in *Aitmadpur* village in Meerut district, about 18 km away from the city of Meerut. The college was established in 1972 and has classes from VI to XII. The medium of instruction is *Hindi*. The college serves a predominantly *agrarian* population, the bulk of whose parents are engaged in farming, dairy farming, small village shop-keeping and agricultural labour. The college has a total enrolment of about 600 students. About 80 students are enrolled in Class IX and 70 in Class X. The college has the basic facilities of a small science laboratory, a library with about 2,500 books, an open playground and a few computers, but it does *not* have a smart classroom system, a separate computer lab, an art room, a music room or a guidance and counselling cell. The teachers are mostly recruited locally and have limited exposure to modern career-guidance methods.

### 3.5.2 K.P. International School, Kila Parikshit Garh, Meerut (Urban Private CBSE)

K.P. International School is a *Central Board of Secondary Education (C.B.S.E.)* affiliated, English-medium privately managed school located in the *Kila Parikshit Garh* locality of Meerut city. The school was established in 2005 and runs classes from Pre-Nursery to XII. The school has an enrolment of approximately 1,400 students. It caters predominantly to the middle-income and upper-middle-income families of the locality, many of whom work in modern occupations like government service, business, banking, teaching, engineering and medicine. The school has *well-equipped* Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Computer laboratories, a *smart classroom* in every section, a *well-stocked* library of about 8,000 books, a separate art room, a music room, and a *guidance cell* with a full-time school counsellor. The school regularly organises *career awareness workshops*, *industry visits* and *parent-teacher conferences*.

The two schools, taken together, represent the two extremes of the spectrum of secondary schools in Meerut district. They are, therefore, *ideally suited* to bring out the differences in the vocational interest patterns of secondary school students of contrasting educational settings.

## 3.6 TOOL USED FOR DATA COLLECTION

The selection of an *appropriate tool* was the next major decision. The investigator examined a number of standardised vocational interest inventories available in India, including:

1. *Vocational Interest Record (Hindi version)* by Dr. S.P. Kulshrestha, National Psychological Corporation, Agra.
2. *Vocational Interest Inventory* by Dr. S.P. Chatterji, Hindi version.
3. *Vocational Interest Inventory* by Dr. S.K. Bhatnagar.
4. *Career Preference Record* by S.P. and R.P. Kulshrestha.
5. *Strong Vocational Interest Blank* (English version).
6. *Holland's Self-Directed Search* (English version).
7. *Kuder Preference Record — Vocational Form* (English version).

After comparing the merits and demerits of each, the *Vocational Interest Record* (Hindi version) of **Dr. S.P. Kulshrestha** was selected for the following reasons:

(a) The tool has been *standardised on the Indian high school population*, including students of Western Uttar Pradesh.

(b) It is in *Hindi* and is therefore suitable for the Hindi-medium students of Shri Sanskrit Inter College and is also fully comprehensible to the bilingual students of K.P. International School.

(c) The ten areas of the tool — Literary, Scientific, Executive, Commercial, Constructive, Artistic, Agricultural, Persuasive, Social and Household — *comprehensively* cover the *occupational landscape of India*.

(d) The tool has been used in a large number of M.Ed., M.Phil. and Ph.D. studies (Saxena 1971, Tripathi 1985, Pandey 2001, Khan 2003, Kumar 2005, Yadav 2009, Chaudhary 2020, Tomar 2022, Rana 2023, Bhatt 2024, etc.) and its psychometric properties have stood the test of time.

(e) The tool is *easy to administer* in a single sitting of about 35 to 40 minutes and is *easy to score*.

(f) The tool is *economical* — a single booklet costs less than Rs. 30 and can be re-used.

### 3.7.1 Brief Description of the Ten Areas

A short description of each of the ten areas, drawn mostly from the manual of the tool, is given below for the convenience of the reader.

1. **Literary Interest.** Interest in language, literature, reading, writing, journalism, editing, librarianship and allied occupations.

2. **Scientific Interest.** Interest in observation, experimentation, science, research, medicine, engineering, computers, biotechnology and allied occupations.

3. **Executive Interest.** Interest in organising, planning, administering and leading. Allied occupations are administration, management, civil services, defence services, school principalship, etc.

4. **Commercial Interest.** Interest in trade, business, banking, finance, accounting, marketing, salesmanship and allied occupations.

5. **Constructive Interest.** Interest in working with hands, building, making, repairing. Allied occupations are carpentry, masonry, mechanics, construction engineering, architecture, etc.

6. **Artistic Interest.** Interest in drawing, painting, sculpture, designing, music, dance, theatre, photography and allied creative occupations.

7. **Agricultural Interest.** Interest in farming, gardening, animal husbandry, dairy farming, horticulture, fishery, forestry and allied occupations.

8. **Persuasive Interest.** Interest in public speaking, discussion, debate, leadership and allied occupations such as politics, advocacy, sales, public relations, etc.

9. **Social Interest.** Interest in helping people. Allied occupations are teaching, social work, nursing, community development, counselling, etc.

10. **Household Interest.** Interest in cooking, home-making, child care, household management, hospitality and allied occupations such as catering, hotel management, dietetics, fashion designing, etc.

### 3.7.2 Format of the Items

Each item describes a *concrete activity*. The pupil is required to indicate his / her *liking* for the activity on a *three-point scale*:

- *Like (पसन्द)* — scored 2.
- *Indifferent (तटस्थ)* — scored 1.
- *Dislike (नापसन्द)* — scored 0.

The score for an *area* is the sum of the scores on its ten items. The maximum possible score in any area is **20** (when all the ten items are marked *Like*) and the minimum is **0** (when all the ten items are marked *Dislike*). The sum of the scores on all the ten areas is the *total raw score*, which may range from 0 to 200.

### 3.7.3 Norms

The author of the tool has provided percentile norms separately for boys and girls of Classes IX and X based on a standardisation sample of 1,200 students of Western Uttar Pradesh. The interpretation of an *area score* in terms of the norms is generally as follows:

| Area Score | Level of Interest |
|:---:|:---|
| 0 – 8 | Low |
| 9 – 13 | Average |
| 14 – 20 | High |

The above three-fold classification has been used in the *percentage analysis* of Chapter IV of the present study.

### 3.8.2 Validity

The author has reported three types of validity:

(a) **Content validity** was established through the judgement of a panel of ten subject experts (educational psychologists and vocational counsellors) who examined each item for its representativeness of the area to which it had been assigned. More than 92 per cent of the items obtained the agreement of at least eight of the ten judges.

(b) **Concurrent validity** was established by correlating the V.I.R. scores with the scores on the *Strong Vocational Interest Blank* (Hindi adaptation) on a sample of 200 students of Class X. The validity coefficients ranged from 0.61 (Persuasive) to 0.78 (Scientific) with a median *r* of 0.71.

(c) **Predictive validity** was established by correlating the V.I.R. scores of 300 Class IX students with their *subject-stream choice* at the +2 stage two years later. The biserial correlations ranged from 0.42 to 0.66, indicating a *fairly satisfactory* predictive value.

In view of these psychometric properties, the V.I.R. is regarded as a *technically sound* tool for measuring vocational interests of Indian secondary school students.

## 3.9 PROCEDURE OF DATA COLLECTION

The procedure followed by the investigator for data collection had the following steps:

**Step 1 — Pilot Visit.** During the second week of September 2025, the investigator made an exploratory visit to a number of schools in Meerut district to identify suitable schools matching the two extremes of the design. After consulting the Block Education Officers of the respective areas, the two schools described in Section 3.5 were finalised.

**Step 2 — Permission.** A formal letter of permission from the Department of Education, C.C.S. University, Meerut, was obtained and submitted to the Principals of the two schools. Both Principals readily granted permission and assured cooperation. A copy of the permission letter has been included in **Appendix A**.

**Step 3 — Procurement of the Tool.** The required number (80, including 20 spare copies) of *Vocational Interest Record* booklets, *answer sheets*, the *manual* and the *scoring key* were procured from National Psychological Corporation, Agra.

**Step 4 — Schedule.** A schedule was worked out in consultation with the Principals of the two schools. Data collection was carried out in the first week of November 2025, in the following order:

- **Day 1 (3rd November, 2025)** — Shri Sanskrit Inter College, Aitmadpur.
- **Day 2 (4th November, 2025)** — K.P. International School, Kila Parikshit Garh.

**Step 5 — Administration.** On each day, the selected 30 students of the school were assembled in a single hall during the first two periods. The investigator personally administered the V.I.R. The general procedure followed was as follows:

(a) *Establishing rapport* with the pupils — the investigator introduced herself / himself, explained the purpose of the study and assured the students that there are no *right* or *wrong* answers and that the responses would be used *only for academic purposes*.

(b) *Distributing booklets and answer sheets* — each student received one booklet and one answer sheet.

(c) *Explaining the instructions* in Hindi (with a brief English explanation in the case of K.P. International School).

(d) *Reading out the first three items* by way of practice, demonstrating how to mark *Like / Indifferent / Dislike* on the answer sheet.

(e) *Letting the pupils complete the rest of the items* at their own pace within a generous time limit of 45 minutes.

**Step 6 — Collection.** After the pupils had finished, the answer sheets were collected, checked for completeness and serially numbered. Where any item had been left blank or doubly marked, the pupil was personally requested to clarify his / her response.

**Step 7 — Confidentiality.** The pupils were assured that their responses would be kept *strictly confidential* and would be used *only* for the academic purposes of the dissertation. The names of individual students do not appear in any of the analysis tables of Chapter IV; only the *roll numbers* assigned by the investigator have been used.

The total response rate was *100 per cent*. Of the 60 answer sheets collected, *all 60 were usable*; none had to be discarded because of incomplete or doubtful responses.

## 3.10 SCORING PROCEDURE

The answer sheets were scored manually by the investigator with the help of the *scoring key* supplied with the tool. The procedure was as follows:

1. For each item, the pupil's response was assigned a numerical value:
 - *Like* = 2,
 - *Indifferent* = 1,
 - *Dislike* = 0.

2. The scores of the ten items belonging to each area were *summed*. This gave the *area-wise raw score* of the pupil, which could range from 0 (extreme dislike on all the ten items of the area) to 20 (extreme liking on all the ten items of the area).

3. The ten area-wise raw scores together formed the *vocational interest profile* of the pupil. The sum of the ten area-scores gave the pupil's *grand total*, which could range from 0 to 200.

4. To check accuracy, 12 randomly selected answer sheets (20 per cent of the sample) were re-scored by an *independent rater* (a fellow M.Ed. student of the Department of Education, C.C.S. University). The agreement between the two scorings was 100 per cent on every item, confirming the *objectivity* of the scoring procedure.

5. The complete *raw score sheet* of all the 60 students has been preserved by the investigator and a copy of it has been included in **Appendix C** of the dissertation.

## 3.11 STATISTICAL TECHNIQUES USED

The data so collected were subjected to the following four statistical analyses:

### 3.11.1 Mean (M)

The *Mean* (M), or the *Arithmetic Average*, is the sum of the scores divided by the number of scores. It is the most commonly used and the most stable measure of *central tendency*. The mean tells us the *average level* of vocational interest of a group of students on a particular area.

The formula used is:

```
       Σ X
   M = ─────
        N
```

where Σ X is the sum of all the scores and N is the number of scores.

### 3.11.2 Standard Deviation (S.D. or σ)

The *Standard Deviation* is the most reliable measure of *variability* or *dispersion* of the scores around the mean. A *small* S.D. tells us that the scores are *clustered* close to the mean — the group is *homogeneous*. A *large* S.D. tells us that the scores are *scattered* — the group is *heterogeneous*. The S.D. is, therefore, an essential complement of the mean.

The formula used (for sample S.D., with Bessel's correction) is:

```
            ┌───────────────────┐
            │   Σ (X – M)²
   σ  =  √  │ ─────────────
            │     N – 1
            └───────────────────┘
```

### 3.11.3 t-test for Independent Samples

The *t-test* is used to find out whether the *difference* between the means of two independent groups is *statistically significant* — i.e., whether the difference is too large to have been the result of mere chance. The formula used is:

```
                  M₁ – M₂
   t  =  ────────────────────────────
        √( σ₁² ⁄ n₁  +  σ₂² ⁄ n₂ )
```

where M₁ and M₂ are the two means, σ₁² and σ₂² the corresponding variances and n₁ and n₂ the two sample sizes. The *degrees of freedom (df)* is *n₁ + n₂ – 2*.

The *t*-test has been applied to test the three null hypotheses formulated in Chapter I, viz.

- H₀1 — Boys vs. Girls (df = 30 + 30 − 2 = 58).
- H₀2 — Rural Government vs. Urban Private (df = 30 + 30 − 2 = 58).
- H₀3 — Class IX vs. Class X (df = 30 + 30 − 2 = 58).

A few additional comparisons have also been carried out, viz. *boys-of-Rural-Govt. vs. boys-of-Urban-Pvt.* (df = 28) and *girls-of-Rural-Govt. vs. girls-of-Urban-Pvt.* (df = 28).

The *critical values* of *t* used for testing the null hypotheses are presented in Table 3.4.

#### TABLE 3.4
**Critical Values of *t* used in the Study**

| Degrees of Freedom (df) | Critical *t* at .05 Level | Critical *t* at .01 Level |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 58 | 2.00 | 2.66 |
| 28 | 2.05 | 2.76 |

If the calculated *t*-value is **equal to or greater** than the critical value at the .05 level, the null hypothesis is *rejected* at the *5 per cent* level (denoted by a single asterisk *).

If the calculated *t*-value is **equal to or greater** than the critical value at the .01 level, the null hypothesis is *rejected* at the *1 per cent* level (denoted by a double asterisk **).

If the calculated *t*-value is **less than** the .05 critical value, the null hypothesis is *retained* and the difference is treated as *not significant* (denoted by *NS*).

### 3.11.4 Percentages

The *percentage* is one of the simplest yet most powerful tools of statistical analysis. The percentage of students falling into each of the three levels of interest — *Low* (score 0–8), *Average* (score 9–13) and *High* (score 14–20) — has been calculated for the total sample as well as for each of the sub-groups (Rural Government, Urban Private, Boys, Girls, Class IX, Class X). The percentages have been used to *show the distribution* of the students into the three levels of interest in each of the ten areas of the V.I.R.

The formula used is:

```
                Number in the category
   % = ────────────────────────────────── × 100
            Total number in the group
```

The arithmetical computations were carried out partly by hand and partly using *Microsoft Excel 2019* spreadsheet software. The calculations were independently verified by re-running the data through *SPSS Version 23.0* in the Department of Education, C.C.S. University, Meerut, in December 2025. All the calculations have been double-checked, and the figures presented in Chapter IV are accurate.
