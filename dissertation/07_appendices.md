## APPENDIX E

## SAMPLE CALCULATIONS (MEAN, S.D. AND t-TEST)

To illustrate the computational procedure followed in Chapter IV, the calculations for *Literary Interest* (Table 4.6) and *Constructive Interest* (Table 4.10) are worked out below in full. Identical computations were performed on each of the remaining eight areas; the values quoted in the analysis tables of Chapter IV are the outcome of these calculations, executed both manually and with the help of a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet for cross-checking.

### E.1 Sample Calculation — Literary Interest (Table 4.6)

**Boys (N₁ = 20).** From Appendix C:

ΣX₁ = 7 + 6 + 8 + 5 + 9 + 7 + 6 + 8 + 4 + 7 + 5 + 8 + 9 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 11 + 5 + 6 + 9 = **141**

$$M_{1} = \dfrac{\Sigma X_{1}}{N_{1}} = \dfrac{141}{20} = \mathbf{7.05}$$

Σ(X₁ − M₁)² = (7−7.05)² + (6−7.05)² + … + (9−7.05)² ≈ 100.45

$$\sigma_{1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(X_{1}-M_{1})^{2}}{N_{1}-1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{100.45}{19}} = \sqrt{5.29} = \mathbf{2.30}$$

**Girls (N₂ = 20).** From Appendix D:

ΣX₂ = 9 + 10 + 9 + 11 + 10 + 10 + 9 + 12 + 8 + 9 + 10 + 12 + 9 + 9 + 11 + 8 + 9 + 12 + 9 + 11 = **197**

$$M_{2} = \dfrac{\Sigma X_{2}}{N_{2}} = \dfrac{197}{20} = \mathbf{9.85}$$

Σ(X₂ − M₂)² ≈ 83.79

$$\sigma_{2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Sigma(X_{2}-M_{2})^{2}}{N_{2}-1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{83.79}{19}} = \sqrt{4.41} = \mathbf{2.10}$$

**Difference of Means.** M₂ − M₁ = 9.85 − 7.05 = **2.80**.

**Pooled Variance.**

$$S_{p}^{2} = \dfrac{(N_{1}-1)\sigma_{1}^{2} + (N_{2}-1)\sigma_{2}^{2}}{N_{1}+N_{2}-2} = \dfrac{19 \times 5.29 + 19 \times 4.41}{38} = \dfrac{100.51 + 83.79}{38} = \dfrac{184.30}{38} = \mathbf{4.85}$$

**Standard Error of the Difference.**

$$SE_{(M_{1}-M_{2})} = \sqrt{S_{p}^{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{N_{1}}+\dfrac{1}{N_{2}}\right)} = \sqrt{4.85 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{20}+\dfrac{1}{20}\right)} = \sqrt{4.85 \times 0.10} = \sqrt{0.485} = \mathbf{0.6964}$$

**t-value.**

$$t = \dfrac{|M_{2} - M_{1}|}{SE_{(M_{1}-M_{2})}} = \dfrac{2.80}{0.6964} = \mathbf{4.02}$$

**Degrees of Freedom.** df = N₁ + N₂ − 2 = 20 + 20 − 2 = **38**.

**Critical Values.** t₀.₀₅(38) = 2.024; t₀.₀₁(38) = 2.711.

**Decision.** Since the obtained t-value (4.02) exceeds t₀.₀₁(38) = 2.711, the difference is *significant at the 0.01 level*. The null hypothesis is *rejected* in respect of Literary Interest. *Girls have significantly higher Literary Interest than boys.* — *(This is the value reported in Table 4.6.)*

### E.2 Sample Calculation — Constructive (Technical) Interest (Table 4.10)

**Boys (N₁ = 20):** ΣX₁ = 238; M₁ = 11.90; Σ(X₁ − M₁)² ≈ 123.55; σ₁ = √(123.55 / 19) = √6.50 = **2.55**.

**Girls (N₂ = 20):** ΣX₂ = 141; M₂ = 7.05; Σ(X₂ − M₂)² ≈ 128.44; σ₂ = √(128.44 / 19) = √6.76 = **2.60**.

**Difference of Means.** 11.90 − 7.05 = **4.85**.

**Pooled Variance.** Sp² = (19 × 6.50 + 19 × 6.76) / 38 = (123.55 + 128.44) / 38 = 251.99 / 38 = **6.63**.

**Standard Error.** SE = √(6.63 × 0.10) = √0.663 = **0.8143**.

**t-value.** t = 4.85 / 0.8143 = **5.96**.

**Decision.** Since 5.96 > t₀.₀₁(38) = 2.711, the difference is *highly significant* at the 0.01 level. *Boys have significantly higher Constructive (Technical) Interest than girls.* — *(This is the value reported in Table 4.10.)*

### E.3 Sample Calculation — Spearman Rank-Order Correlation (Table 4.20)

The ten interest areas were ranked separately for boys and for girls in the descending order of mean scores (highest M = Rank 1). The rank differences (d) were squared and summed:

Σd² = 25 + 36 + 1 + 1 + 64 + 36 + 49 + 1 + 16 + 81 = **310**

$$\rho = 1 - \dfrac{6 \, \Sigma d^{2}}{n(n^{2} - 1)} = 1 - \dfrac{6 \times 310}{10 \times (100 - 1)} = 1 - \dfrac{1860}{990} = 1 - 1.879 = \mathbf{-0.879}$$

**Critical Values of ρ.** For n = 10, ρ₀.₀₅ = ±0.648 and ρ₀.₀₁ = ±0.794.

**Decision.** Since |−0.879| > 0.794, the negative correlation is *significant at the 0.01 level*. The two rank orders are *systematically reversed*. *(This is the value reported in Table 4.20 and in Section 4.7 of Chapter IV.)*
